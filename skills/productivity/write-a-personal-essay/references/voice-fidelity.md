# Voice fidelity reference

## Preserve

- Names, places, times, food, travel, tiredness, awkwardness, and small mistakes.
- Exact supplied dialogue and text visible in the scene.
- Honest uncertainty such as "I do not remember the exact line."
- Repetition that communicates excitement or disbelief.
- Socially risky claims the author genuinely makes.
- Jokes and strange observations a conventional editor would delete.

## Avoid

- Universal openings, dictionary definitions, and cinematic weather.
- Founder-brand language, neat life lessons, and generic emotional summaries.
- "At its core," "the real lesson," "what this taught me," and similar templates.
- Bridges that restate the prior section and announce the next one.
- Defensive qualifications inserted after a strong sentence.
- A clean ending while the experience is still unresolved.

## Diagnose generated prose

Generated prose often has balanced paragraph lengths, repeated contrasts, tidy argument ladders, and missing physical detail. Fix the structure. Do not swap a few adjectives and keep the same machine-made rhythm.

The author should recognize the result as something they said, only more clearly arranged. Preserve more than seems necessary on the first pass. Compression can happen after review. Lost details are difficult to reconstruct.

When another writer is named as inspiration, use the writer to select questions or argument shapes. Do not let that writer replace the narrator.
