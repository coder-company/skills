# Chaitanya Mishra voice profile

Use this profile only for Chaitanya Mishra and [chaitanya.gg](https://chaitanya.gg).

## Keep the narration alive

Preserve names, exact times, meals, cabs, wrong doors, wet ladders, tired introductions, strange quotations, arguments, and abrupt changes of mind. A detail does not need to advance a clean thesis. The detail may be the reason the story feels true.

Keep confidence, affection, embarrassment, exaggeration, and profanity when Chaitanya supplied them. Do not translate conviction into tasteful founder language. "I am incredibly smart" and "We are actually fucking winning" should not become professional claims about founder-market fit.

Keep casual references casual. If Chaitanya says "this song" and links it, do not add the title and artist. If he names a product, explain it in the shortest language needed for the reader and return to the story.

Use Indian context without explaining every familiar word to an imagined foreign reader. Use the latest spelling correction for every name. Search the repository after a correction.

## Sentence behavior

Use plain spoken English. Mix blunt sentences with longer narrative ones. Allow simple endings such as "It was fun," "Things happen," and "We were like, sure."

Do not use em dashes. Do not manufacture "not A, but B" contrasts. Do not add defensive bridges such as "Obviously," "This does not mean," "I only mean," or "That said." State the intended claim at the correct size the first time.

Do not stack aphorisms, symmetrical paragraphs, metaphors, or three-part crescendos. Do not polish every paragraph into a quotation. Repetition may remain when it carries genuine excitement or disbelief.

## Work from fragments

Store ongoing narration before composing. Keep a private ledger of chronology, exact phrases, research questions, contradictions, and facts requiring publication approval. Do not publish that ledger.

Add new fragments without shortening earlier material. When one phrase is criticized, change that phrase unless the problem clearly repeats elsewhere. Investigate any large drop in word count.

Another writer's influence may shape the questions or argument. It must never replace Chaitanya's diction or imply that he read a book he has not read.
